GLUTEN-FREE DINNER GUIDELINES AND RECIPES

Following a gluten-free diet is essential for individuals with celiac disease, gluten sensitivity, or wheat allergy. Here are guidelines and recipes for healthy gluten-free dinners:

UNDERSTANDING GLUTEN
Gluten is a protein found in wheat, barley, rye, and triticale. It must be completely avoided by those with celiac disease or gluten sensitivity.

NATURALLY GLUTEN-FREE FOODS
- All fresh fruits and vegetables
- Meat, poultry, and fish (unprocessed)
- Eggs
- Dairy products (plain)
- Legumes: beans, lentils, chickpeas
- Nuts and seeds
- Rice, quinoa, corn
- Potatoes and sweet potatoes

GLUTEN-FREE GRAINS AND STARCHES
- Rice (all varieties)
- Quinoa
- Corn and cornmeal
- Buckwheat
- Millet
- Amaranth
- Oats (certified gluten-free)
- Potatoes
- Sweet potatoes

SAMPLE GLUTEN-FREE DINNER RECIPES

GRILLED SALMON WITH QUINOA AND VEGETABLES
- 6 oz salmon fillet
- 1 cup cooked quinoa
- 1 cup roasted vegetables (asparagus, zucchini, bell peppers)
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- Lemon wedges
- Fresh herbs

Salmon provides omega-3 fatty acids and protein. Quinoa is a complete protein and naturally gluten-free grain.

CHICKEN AND VEGETABLE STIR-FRY WITH RICE
- 6 oz chicken breast, sliced
- 2 cups mixed vegetables
- 1 cup cooked brown rice
- 2 tablespoons gluten-free tamari sauce
- 1 tablespoon sesame oil
- Ginger and garlic

Use tamari instead of regular soy sauce (which contains wheat). Serve over brown rice for a complete meal.

STUFFED BELL PEPPERS
- 4 bell peppers
- 1 cup cooked quinoa
- 1/2 cup black beans
- 1/2 cup corn
- 1/4 cup diced tomatoes
- 1/4 cup shredded cheese (optional)
- Spices: cumin, chili powder

A filling and nutritious gluten-free dinner option. Provides protein, fiber, and various vitamins.

ZUCCHINI NOODLES WITH MEATBALLS
- 2 large zucchinis, spiralized
- 4-6 gluten-free meatballs
- 1 cup marinara sauce (check label for gluten)
- Fresh basil
- Parmesan cheese (optional)

Zucchini noodles are a great pasta alternative. Use certified gluten-free meatballs or make your own.

READING FOOD LABELS
- Check for "gluten-free" certification
- Look for hidden sources: modified food starch, malt, soy sauce
- Be aware of cross-contamination risks
- When in doubt, contact the manufacturer

CROSS-CONTAMINATION PREVENTION
- Use separate cutting boards and utensils
- Clean surfaces thoroughly
- Store gluten-free foods separately
- Use dedicated toaster for gluten-free bread
- Be cautious when dining out

NUTRITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS
- Gluten-free diets can be low in fiber (add whole grains like quinoa, brown rice)
- May be low in B vitamins (consider fortified foods or supplements)
- Ensure adequate iron intake
- Include a variety of naturally gluten-free whole foods
